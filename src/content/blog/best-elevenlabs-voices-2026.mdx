Voice selection is the most underrated decision in AI audio production. The difference between ElevenLabs' best and worst voices for a given use case is larger than most people expect — and spending 10 minutes on this decision before starting a project saves hours of rework and produces noticeably better output.

This guide covers the best voices for each major use case, based on testing across real production scenarios.

</InfoCallout>

## Best Voices for YouTube Narration

**Rachel** — The most consistently recommended ElevenLabs voice for English-language YouTube. American accent, clear and warm, with natural pacing that works across a wide range of content types: explainers, listicles, documentaries, and commentary. Rachel does not draw attention to itself — it lets the script do the work, which is exactly what good narration should do.

**Adam** — The most popular male voice for AI and tech YouTube channels. Deep, authoritative, and direct. Works best for content where the narrator needs to sound confident and knowledgeable: analysis videos, reviews, tutorials. Slightly more formal than Rachel — better for structured, informational content than casual vlogs.

**Josh** — A warmer, more approachable male alternative to Adam. Better for personal finance, self-improvement, and lifestyle content where the narrator should feel like a knowledgeable peer rather than a remote authority.

**Model recommendation:** v3 for emotional storytelling and character-driven content. v2 for structured, factual narration where consistency matters more than expression.

## Best Voices for Podcasts

**Cassidy** — ElevenLabs' best voice for conversational podcast narration. American female, natural cadence, sounds like someone you would actually listen to for 30 minutes. Handles longer paragraphs well without feeling robotic on second or third sentence.

**Josh** — Doubles well as a podcast voice. The warmth that makes it good for YouTube works equally well for podcast audio where listeners are wearing headphones and the intimacy of the voice matters more than it does through a laptop speaker.

**Charlotte** — British female, measured and articulate. Well-suited to interview-style podcast narration, current affairs, and any content format where a more formal register works in your favour.

**Tip:** For podcasts specifically, test your chosen voice at 1.0 and 0.95 speed — slightly slowed delivery sounds more natural in audio-first formats than on video where visual movement sets pace.

## Best Voices for Audiobooks

**James** — A deep, composed British male voice. The standout ElevenLabs voice for audiobooks — evocative, well-suited to fiction narration and history, with a gravitas that holds attention over long listening sessions. Particularly effective for historical fiction, biographical narrative, and anything with a documentary quality.

**Charlotte** — A strong female alternative for audiobooks. Clear, precise, and handles complex prose well. Better for non-fiction and literary fiction than for character-heavy genre fiction where range is more important than consistency.

**Rachel** — Works well for narrative non-fiction audiobooks where the tone should be warm and approachable rather than formal. Popular for personal development, memoir, and business books.

**Model recommendation:** v3 for fiction with emotional range. v2 for non-fiction where consistent, controlled delivery is more important than expressiveness.

## Best Voices for Ads and Marketing Videos

**Bella** — Warm, upbeat, and persuasive. ElevenLabs' best voice for female-led ad voiceover. Sounds genuinely enthusiastic without tipping into the over-the-top register that makes AI voices feel synthetic. Works across product demo videos, social ads, and explainer content.

**Antoni** — The best male ElevenLabs voice for commercial content. Smooth and professional, with a delivery that suits B2B and consumer advertising equally. Lower stakes than Adam — less authoritative, more likeable.

**Tip for ads:** Run the v3 model at moderate stability (0.5–0.65) for marketing content. Lower stability adds the natural variation that makes copy feel delivered rather than read — which is the difference between audio that converts and audio that sounds synthetic.

## Best Voices for Short-Form Video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts)

**Natasha (Valley Girl)** — ElevenLabs' most generated voice by volume in 2026, and the most recognisable sound on AI-narrated TikTok and YouTube Shorts. Energetic, attention-commanding, and fast-paced by default. If your content format needs to capture attention in the first two seconds, this is the voice that performs.

**Countdown Casey** — Specifically designed for countdown and list-format videos. If you are producing "Top 10" or ranking content, this voice is built for the format in a way that general voices are not.

**Tip:** For short-form content, run v3 at slightly lower stability than you would for long-form. Short-form audiences are more accustomed to variation in delivery; higher stability sounds flat at short duration.
